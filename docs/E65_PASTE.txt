## READ THIS FIRST - FOUR RULES, AND THE FIRST ONE IS NOW CODE

The last Phase A run shipped 25 objects that concluded nothing. It was accepted, then
measured, and the measurement is why these four rules exist. Three are earned. One is now
enforced by the pipeline whether or not you agree with it.

### 1. Every structural field is answered, or the object is retired automatically

`clab.external.batch_health.enforce()` runs against the store after ingest.

  - An object answering **ZERO** of the three structural ordinals - `structural_growth`,
    `replication_difficulty`, `substitution_risk` - is marked SUPERSEDED with no
    replacement. Automatically. The companies that resolve to it then show as having no
    current industry object, which is the honest state and is worse than nothing shipped.
  - An object answering **one or two** survives, and is reported as `below_ship_bar` for
    you to justify. It is not deleted, because a reasoned UNKNOWN is a result: E53's
    utilities returned `market_share_direction` UNKNOWN 59 times out of 59 and were
    correct, since a rate-regulated monopoly has no contestable share.
  - The ship bar is 3 of 3. It is not a new or harsh bar. Measured on the live store
    2026-09-09, after the retirement pass: **49 of 60 active objects clear it** -
    Communication Services 11/11, Utilities 7/7, **IT 5/5**, Real Estate 1/1, Energy 7/8,
    Financials 18/22, Industrials 0/6. The eleven that do not are partials at 1 or 2 of 3,
    kept for the reason above. Industrials shipped 26 and 20 of them are gone.

This is stated so you know what happens, not to make you assert. Asserting an unevidenced
categorical to clear the gate is the other error and it costs the same - see THE TWO
ERRORS below. The gate deletes empty objects; it does not reward invented ones, and every
categorical still needs a claim naming that field or the finalizer strips it.

### 2. An UNKNOWN needs a written reason naming what was looked for

For every field you leave UNKNOWN, the object's report entry must carry:

  - the specific evidence you went looking for (name the document, filing item, statistic
    or regulator - "no data" is not a reason),
  - where you looked and what came back,
  - and which of the two it was: **evidence genuinely absent**, or **you stopped short**.

"I stopped short" is an acceptable answer and we want it recorded. What is not acceptable
is a blank. This rule is what separates the two cases the gate cannot tell apart, and
without it a below-bar object cannot be reviewed at all.

### 3. Depth follows the evidence - a uniform claim count is a quota, and a quota has produced a defect twice

E54 returned exactly 5 claims per company. E57 returned exactly one claim per field. Both
uniformities were the artifact, not the finding: E57 passed 66 of 66 literal quote checks
while a later audit found only 11 passages directly established their assigned field, and
all 11 Health Care objects were retired.

A well-documented industry with three regulators and a trade association should carry more
claims than a six-company niche with two public filers. If your claim counts come back
near-identical across twelve structurally different industries, that is a signal you
allocated by budget instead of by evidence, and you should say so.

Aim for at least eight field-specific claims and three genuinely independent organizations
per object. **Aim** is exact: if the evidence is not there, return fewer claims and say
why. Do not pad, and do not manufacture a domain.

### 4. Context is not support, and source diversity is part of the deliverable

Measured on the live corpus today:

  - **`stance` is currently a per-run labelling habit, not a property of the evidence, and
    that is our defect rather than yours.** Across 508 industry-object claims: Health Care
    filed 63 SUPPORTS and 0 CONTEXT, Communication Services 44 and 11, IT 22 and 4 - while
    Financials filed 29 SUPPORTS against 103 CONTEXT and Industrials 11 against 134. Three
    sectors never filed a single FACT claim; one never filed an INFERENCE. A field that is
    ~100% one value in one run and ~100% the other in the next is measuring the run.
    Because of that we cannot presently tell context-only evidence from unlabelled
    support, which is exactly why the two labels below are demanded explicitly.
  - Of the 26 objects the Industrials tree shipped, **20 have ZERO claims labelled
    SUPPORTS**, and those same 20 are exactly the 20 that answered 0 of 3 and have since
    been auto-retired. Do not over-read it: eleven Financials objects also carry zero
    SUPPORTS labels **and answer all three fields**, so the label alone does not separate a
    good object from an empty one (measured 2026-09-09, `journal/experiments/E78_results.md`).
    The claim that survives both is narrower and still worth acting on: **an object with no
    passage that actually supports a field has nothing that can answer one.**
  - **25 of those 26 objects use a single independence domain.** 140 of 145 claims are
    `COMPETITOR_FILING`. Independent issuers give organization diversity; they do not give
    source-type diversity, and confidence in this system counts distinct domains.

Two mechanical traps that produced part of the above:

  - `stance` **defaults to CONTEXT** when the field is omitted from a claim, and
    `fact_or_inference` defaults to INFERENCE. Set both explicitly on every claim. A claim
    you meant as support but left unlabelled is stored as context.
  - `independence_domain` is a label, so it is a lever. `research_ingest.evidence_profile()`
    checks it structurally: claims vs distinct URLs vs distinct hosts vs distinct domains,
    how many domains rest on a single claim, and whether one host was filed under two
    domains. On an earlier delivery it caught `www.se.com` entered as both
    `COMPETITOR_FILING` and `SUPPLIER`, manufacturing a fourth domain out of one
    organization. Do not relabel to diversify. Find another organization, or report the
    concentration.

`evidence_profile()` output per object is a required part of your report.

## ONE FIELD, ONE SEMANTIC RECEIPT

Quote containment is necessary and not sufficient. E57 passed 66 of 66 literal quote checks
while a field-by-field audit found only 11 passages directly supported the complete field
claim - the builder had assigned each of three source windows to a fixed pair of fields.

Do not create a `FIELD_SOURCE_INDEX` or any equivalent pairing shortcut.

- One claim backs one field.
- Never attach an identical quote/excerpt window to two fields. The expected count of
  reused windows in your report is zero.
- The same URL may back two claims only when two genuinely different passages establish
  two different facts; create separate claims and explain the distinction.
- For each non-UNKNOWN categorical, provide a claim whose passage directly supports that
  categorical's field, scope and direction - and whose `stance` is SUPPORTS.
- Support the material assertions in `market_structure`, `moat_mechanism`,
  `technology_trajectory`, `regulatory_trajectory` and `key_metrics` with field-specific
  receipts. A generic company description is not a replication-difficulty receipt; an
  export-control proposal is not a realized outcome; a metric used by one segment is not
  an industry metric without a scope argument.
- Compare peer metrics only on the same period, definition and economic basis. If the
  bases differ, say so and do not rank them.
- Distinguish proposed, approved, implemented and realized events. This matters more in IT
  than in the sectors done so far: export controls, entity-list additions and the EU AI Act
  each have a proposal date, an effective date and a compliance date, and they are not the
  same event.

## THE TWO ERRORS COST THE SAME

There are two ways to be wrong here and they are equally bad.

  ASSERTING what you cannot evidence.   A categorical with no claim naming that field is
                                        removed by the finalizer. It corrupts the score
                                        and it wastes the search that produced it.

  ABSTAINING when you could have        An UNKNOWN on a field that the evidence would
  established it.                       have supported is a silent loss. It costs the
                                        company real points - the score divides by what
                                        APPLIES, not by what you answered - and unlike a
                                        bad assertion nothing in the pipeline catches it.

Neither is the safe choice. UNKNOWN is not a way to avoid being wrong; it is a different
way to be wrong, and it is the one that leaves no trace.

For every field you leave UNKNOWN, say in the report which you were closer to: was the
evidence genuinely absent, or did you stop short? An honest "I stopped short" is more
useful to us than a confident blocking claim, and it costs you nothing.

## WHAT UNKNOWN MEANS

UNKNOWN means the evidence to establish this field does not exist or could not be
reached. It does NOT mean:

  - the answer is uncertain          -> give the answer your evidence supports
  - the answer is mid-range          -> MODERATE and STABLE are real values, use them
  - peers are hard to compare        -> say so AND give your best-evidenced reading
  - the field feels risky to answer  -> that is the abstention error, see above

UNKNOWN scores as NO_DATA and never as zero, so it does not drag a company down relative
to its peers. That is exactly why it is tempting and exactly why it must be earned.

## CITATIONS ARE REQUIRED, AND THEY ARE NOT A REASON TO ABSTAIN

Every non-UNKNOWN categorical needs a claim whose `field` names it. That rule exists to
stop unbacked assertion, NOT to discourage answering. If you can evidence a field, cite
it and answer it. If the citation requirement is what is stopping you from answering a
field you believe you could establish, say so explicitly in the report - that is a
finding about our process and we want it.

## CLAIM AND SOURCE RULES

1. No score, rank, rating, price target or gate. Company Lab computes every number.
2. Every claim needs `source_url`, `source_date`, a verbatim quote no longer than 300
   characters, and an excerpt actually read. The quote must appear literally in the
   excerpt.
3. Use primary filings, regulators, official statistics, standards bodies and credible
   competitor disclosures where available. A company's IR is the weakest source. IT has
   unusually good non-issuer sources - BIS and the Federal Register for export controls,
   the FCC and ITU for spectrum and standards, SEMI and WSTS for semiconductor statistics,
   USPTO and ETSI for essential patents, NIST and CISA for security requirements, the EU AI
   Act official texts. Use them; they are how this sector reaches three independence
   domains without relabelling anything.
4. One organization gets one `independence_domain`. A company, its IR site and its filing
   are not independent organizations.
5. Never invent a number, date, source, quote, competitor or search count.
6. UNKNOWN is NO_DATA, never zero.
7. Every object and the sector object have `status: "SHADOW"`.
8. Do not optimize for a verification rate. Report literal containment and semantic
   support separately, with exact denominators.
