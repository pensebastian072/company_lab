## The bar — what makes an industry object an expert

1. All three structural ordinals answered (`structural_growth`, `replication_difficulty`,
   `substitution_risk`) — the ship bar.
2. Every non-UNKNOWN field backed by a claim passing **exact containment** on its cited page.
3. At least one **non-issuer** source (regulator, trade body, statistical agency, academic).
4. `structural_growth` from a **published industry quantity**, never company revenue,
   employment or a sentiment index. **Exception, authorised by the user 2026-09-12** ("just
   use their revenues since they are similar companies"): where **no** trade body, regulator
   or statistical agency publishes a quantity covering the unit, the ordinal may come from
   the sum of the listed members' own filed revenue, deflated by CPI, through
   `clab/external/industry_growth.py`. It is the weakest ordinal in the corpus — the
   quantity is partly an output of the companies being scored, and a one-year nominal window
   reads a cycle as a structure — so it carries four guards (≥4 peers, no member over 50% of
   the aggregate, aggregate within 5 points of the median member, structural revenue moves
   excluded), its claim is labelled `COMPETITOR_FILING` rather than `PRIMARY_DATA` so it
   cannot satisfy rule 3, and `scripts/apply_industry_growth.py` **refuses to run without an
   explicit `--units` list**. A real published quantity replaces it the moment one is found;
   it can never replace a researched value.
   **Narrowed the same day by E84:** the ordinal must also be **stable across two windows**
   (1-year and 3-year, via `agreeing_growth`) because the two disagreed on 8 of the 13 units
   either could answer, and on 3 of the 5 already written — `leisure_products` read FLAT at one
   year and DECLINING at three. Those three were retracted, costing 42 companies of
   eligibility. The exception therefore carries **7 units, not 18**, and the remaining units
   are waiting on a published quantity, not on a wider window. Rule 4 was right.
5. A single-member unit names its private or foreign peers, or is folded.

Gate keeps retiring at **zero** of three. Phase B runs against objects at **≥2 of 3 only**.

### What this session proved about method, which the next passes should assume

1. **Any ordinal computed from one window of market data is unstable until a second window is
   tried.** It cost nothing to test and it retracted 3 of 5 units (E84).
2. **When an agency series and the members' own revenue disagree by several points, the series is
   not scoped to the unit.** The direction of the gap says which is wrong: aerospace had the
   agency lower and the agency was right; machinery and motor vehicles had the agency higher and
   the members were right (E87, E88).
3. **A table is citable when a row carries levels beside its percent change** — the arithmetic
   then identifies the columns. Percentages alone with a lost header are not citable (Fed G.17),
   and neither is a chart whose labels and values separate (BEA PCE).
4. **A source that needs a browser user-agent, or that 403s an anonymous client, cannot be a
   citation at all** — it verifies at staging and fails the merge's bare re-fetch. eCFR, BTS,
   AAR and USACE are all in this category; use GPO's CFR XML for regulations.
5. **Growth that arrived with new shares was bought, not earned.** REITs make this the dominant
   confound in Real Estate (E86).

## Correction 2026-09-12 — the E47 half of the Phase 0 criterion was wrong

The criterion below read: *"must ACCEPT E47 `f92abdf0…` and REFUSE E59-b3 `9e243401…` on
`four_field_fill`"*. The second half is now met exactly. **The first half is wrong and has
been struck.**

With `acceptance._payload_rows` fixed (it read `categoricals`; payloads carry `pass_a`, so
every payload scored 0.0% fill with all four rank-order fields "all-UNKNOWN"), the gate
refuses E47 on `constant_fields`, and it is right to. Measured from the payload itself:

```text
E47 f92abdf0, 59 utilities
  competitive_position        59 of 59 answered   LAGGARD 17 · SN2 15 · LEADER 14 · CHAL 13
  competitive_position_trend   0 of 59 answered   UNKNOWN 59
```

The same researcher, on the same companies, in the same batch, placed every one of 59
companies on the competitive ladder and declined 59 times out of 59 to say which way they
were moving. That is the E43–E46 abstention collapse in a single field. Accepting E47 would
require weakening `constant_fields`, which is the one check that exists to stop exactly this.

The criterion was written from the belief that E47 was clean, not from a measurement of it.
Nothing operational changes: Utilities is **0 of 59** live in the store, and Phase 1 already
routes it through E80, not E47. What changes is that replaying E47 to restore Utilities is
now known to import a field that is dead in all 59 rows. Full record:
`journal/experiments/ACCEPTANCE_GATE_FALSE_REJECT_2026-09-11.md`.

## Taxonomy splits — decided 2026-09-12, both declined

Two units have a recorded finding that they are more than one economic object:
`hotels_resorts_cruise_lines` (17 companies — OTAs, hotel brands, timeshare, cruise lines
and a ski operator) and `leisure_products` (9 — marine, powersports, RV, toys, golf,
fitness, drinkware). The user delegated the decision on 2026-09-12. **Neither is being
split**, for three reasons:

1. The standing rule requires **one ordinal-supporting claim in hand per resulting piece**
   before a split, and those claims do not exist. Splitting first would create four or seven
   new zero-claim objects, which is what E61 did and what the retirement sweep then undid.
2. A split re-keys every member's `industry_id`, which invalidates the peer sets that
   `revenue_share` and the peer_* fields are computed against, for a taxonomy change that
   buys no evidence.
3. Both units turned out to be answerable **without** a split. Each now carries a unit-wide
   `substitution_risk` — in both cases because the substitute is diffuse (any other use of
   the same discretionary hour) and therefore genuinely describes every member — and
   `hotels_resorts_cruise_lines` also takes a `structural_growth` from the revenue route
   under rule 4's exception, at a top-member share of 15%, the most diversified unit in the
   book. 17 and 9 companies reached Phase B eligibility with the taxonomy untouched.

The findings stay on the record. If a later pass obtains per-piece claims, the split becomes
cheap and can be reconsidered then.
