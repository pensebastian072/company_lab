Twelve claims, every one from the **USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026**. Seven units
answered; nine skeletons written and left at 0 of 3.

## Why this sector cost a fraction of what Consumer Discretionary cost

The USGS publishes, for every commodity, a physical production quantity **and** an explicit
`Substitutes:` paragraph written by a named government mineral specialist. That is two of the
three ordinals from one non-issuer source, in narrative prose, with no column to guess at.

Compare the three sources that failed this week for exactly that reason:

| source | why it could not be cited |
|---|---|
| BEA PCE "recreational goods and vehicles" | appears only as a chart; extraction yields 16 numbers against 11 labels |
| Fed G.17 industrial production | per-industry rows extract cleanly, the HEADER does not — 13 unattributable columns |
| Census MARTS | usable, but only as a raw table row with runs of dots, sliced by regex rather than typed |

The lesson is about the **form** a source publishes in, not its quality. The Fed's G.17 is a
better instrument for chemicals than anything I found, and it is unusable here because a PDF
table without its header cannot be quoted honestly. `specialty_chemicals` — 23 companies, the
largest unit in the sector — is unanswered for that reason alone.

## The seven units

- **construction_materials** (6) — DECLINING on tons: *"portland and blended cement production
  decreased to an estimated 82 million tons from an estimated 83 million tons"*, masonry cement
  −2.7%. ELEVATED on substitution, where the substitute is **inside the product**: fly ash and
  slag replace clinker in blended cement. Scope stated on the claim — cement is the binder, and
  these six also sell aggregates, whose volumes are not in this number.
- **copper** (1) — DECLINING: mine production *"an estimated 1.0 million tons, a decrease of 5%
  from that in 2024"* while the value of that output rose 10%. **A revenue-based read of this
  industry would have called it growing**, which is the price-versus-volume trap this whole
  week has been about. ELEVATED on the USGS's own substitute list.
- **aluminum** (3) — FLAT: capacity *"unchanged from that in 2024"*, production *"decreased
  slightly"*, two smelters idle since 2022 and 2024. FLAT and not DECLINING because the agency's
  word is "slightly" with no percentage, and the honest reading of a small unquantified decrease
  is the flat band. Value of production +35% on a +39% price move, for contrast.
- **steel** (9) — DECLINING: 82 million tons, and the only year-over-year figure the agency
  gives is in dollars — $149bn from $150bn, −0.7% nominal, about **−4% real** against CPI +3.4%.
  Flagged on the claim itself as a dollar quantity needing a deflator, unlike the cement and
  copper claims in the same batch.
- **gold** (3) — ELEVATED only, 1 of 3. Two substitution channels: direct (palladium, platinum,
  silver) and **thrifting** — gold-clad base metals in products *"continually redesigned to
  maintain high-utility standards with lower gold content"*, which is substitution inside the
  same product. Plus 90 tons of recycled scrap, **about 60% of reported consumption**, competing
  with mined metal. Growth stays UNKNOWN: the summary gives 160 tons for 2025 and no prior-year
  tonnage, and its +32% is value, which is price.
- **silver** (1) — ELEVATED only. The clearest case of **completed** substitution in the sector:
  the agency is describing a demand category digital imaging already took.
- **fertilizers_agricultural_chemicals** (5) — the interesting one, below.

## The first LOW in the corpus

```text
Nitrogen is an essential plant nutrient that has no substitute.
There are no substitutes for phosphorus in agriculture.
```

Both sentences are the USGS's. Two of the three primary nutrients have **no substitute at any
price**, so `substitution_risk` is **LOW** — the first LOW this corpus has recorded, against a
week in which almost every other unit came out ELEVATED. When a measurement keeps returning the
same value it is worth asking whether it can return anything else (the E34 lesson), and this is
the answer: it can, and here it did.

`replication_difficulty` is HIGH on the same sector's geology: *"phosphate rock ore was mined by
five companies at 10 mines in four States."* The barrier cannot be built — phosphate rock exists
where it exists — and the nitrogen half has its own physical version, 57% of ammonia capacity in
Louisiana, Oklahoma and Texas because that is where the cheap gas is. So this unit reaches 2 of 3
without a growth figure at all.

The caveat is on the claim: **absence of a substitute is not absence of competition.** Imported
nitrogen and phosphate compete directly with domestic product. That is a trade question, and this
field does not measure it.

## The chemicals addendum: growth answered, substitution not

The American Chemistry Council publishes a production index, which is a volume measure and
therefore rule-4 compliant without a deflator. `structural_growth` **FLAT** now stands on
*"With the preliminary December data, chemical production was up 1.3% in 2025 compared to
2024"*, recorded against `specialty_chemicals` (23), `diversified_chemicals` (4) and
`commodity_chemicals` (3) — **30 companies**, each now at 1 of 3.

Three things are on the claim rather than left for a reader to find:

- **Window instability again.** The same ACC paragraph gives December alone at **−3.4% Y/Y**,
  the CPRI three-month average at **+0.1% Y/Y**, and calendar 2025 at **+1.3%**. One month, one
  quarter, one year: −3.4%, +0.1%, +1.3%. This is E84's finding arriving from a completely
  different source on the same day, so the widest window is used and the other two are recorded
  on the claim instead of being chosen between silently.
- **Vintage.** This is the most recent ACC report carrying the CPRI; the April 2026 weekly
  report does not publish it. The quantity is calendar 2025, read in September 2026.
- **Scope.** The index is chemical production as a whole and does not separate specialty from
  commodity, so all three units carry the same figure and none gets one specific to itself.

`substitution_risk` was attempted from filings across PPG, SHW, EMN, CE, LYB, DOW and IFF and
**failed honestly.** The only hit was Lyondell's *"customers reduce their consumption or use
substitute products"*, which is generic price-elasticity boilerplate naming no substitute at
all — nothing like VF naming private label or the USGS naming aluminium for copper. Recording it
would have been padding. The three chemicals units stay at 1 of 3 and the sector's remaining
need is a named-substitute source, not another filing sweep.

## Rule 5 debt, recorded

Three of these units are single-member — `copper` (FCX), `silver` (HL), `paper_products`
(SLVM) — and the plan's rule 5 says a single-member unit must name its private or foreign peers
or be folded. None of them does yet. The USGS text names plenty of them (copper is mined in
eight states by 26 mines; the summaries list import sources), so this is cheap to close, but it
is not closed, and those objects would fail rule 5 at an object-level acceptance run the same
way the revenue-route units fail rule 3.
